What a bookkeeper actually does for you

Short answer: A bookkeeper records and categorizes every transaction, reconciles your bank and credit card accounts to the statements, and produces a profit & loss and balance sheet each month. We do that in QuickBooks Online for a fixed fee of $300–750 a month, with payroll, sales tax, and tax returns available from the same firm.

Good books answer the questions you actually have: Did we make money last month? Can I afford to hire? What will I owe in taxes? They also make tax season a non-event, because your preparer starts from numbers that already tie out.

Every month

  • All bank, credit card, and loan accounts reconciled to statements
  • Transactions categorized consistently, with bank rules so nothing drifts
  • Profit & loss and balance sheet by the 10th
  • A short note on what changed, what to watch, and any questions, in one batch

Common questions

What's the difference between a bookkeeper and a CPA?

A bookkeeper keeps the day-to-day financial records and produces monthly financial statements. A CPA is a licensed accountant who can perform audits, reviews, and other attest work. Many small businesses need a bookkeeper every month and a tax preparer once a year; few need attest services. We provide bookkeeping and tax preparation, and coordinate with a CPA when a bank or contract requires a CPA-issued report.

How much does small business bookkeeping cost?

Our fixed monthly fees are $300–350 for Essential, $350–450 for Professional, and $450–750 for Premium, depending on transaction volume and complexity. Payroll and sales tax are add-ons. You get an exact quote after we review your books.

Do I need QuickBooks Online?

Most clients use QuickBooks Online, and we can set it up for you. If you're already on Xero, we can work there too.

No. Monthly plans are month-to-month with 30 days' written notice to cancel.
